# 🦴 Spine Harmony

**An AI agent spec for interactive spine-health Q&A, structured symptom/pain tracking, MRI/DICOM image review workflows, consultation preparation, and patient-facing reports.**

Spine Harmony is a spine-specific AI-agent specification and workspace pattern. It helps individuals ask structured questions about their own spine-health materials, maintain structured symptom and pain diaries, convert user-owned MRI/DICOM files into high-resolution review images for non-clinical organization, prepare for appointments, and draft patient-facing reports for discussion with qualified clinicians.

> **Important safety warning:** Spine Harmony is not medical software, not a medical device, not diagnosis, not treatment guidance, not emergency triage, not radiology interpretation, not clinical decision support, and not a substitute for professional medical care. Do not use it to make or delay medical decisions. For severe, new, progressive, or concerning symptoms, seek qualified medical care or emergency services. See [DISCLAIMER.md](DISCLAIMER.md) and [TERMS.md](TERMS.md).

## What this project is

Spine Harmony provides reusable agent instructions, templates, documentation, and example workflows for building a personal spine-health research assistant around your own files and chosen AI runtime. **It should not be assumed to keep medical data local or private once you use an LLM, vision model, hosted agent runtime, cloud workspace, or other external service.**

It is intended for:

- interactive spine-health Q&A grounded in user-provided notes and source documents;
- structured symptom and pain tracking, including daily check-ins, trend summaries, medication/activity context, flare notes, and post-procedure recovery diaries;
- MRI/DICOM image export from user-owned imaging files into high-resolution review images and contact sheets;
- LLM-assisted imaging review to generate non-clinical observations and better clinician questions, with explicit clinical-verification caveats;
- organizing spine-related history and notes;
- maintaining symptom, pain, activity, medication, sleep, flare, and treatment-response timelines;
- preparing questions for clinician visits;
- comparing information across appointments, imaging summaries, and symptom logs;
- summarizing user-provided medical records for personal organization and clinician discussion;
- drafting patient-facing reports that can be reviewed with qualified clinicians.

## MRI/DICOM image export and assisted review

Spine Harmony's imaging workflow is a core feature. It can help users convert their own MRI/DICOM files into high-resolution review images and contact sheets, then use those images alongside reports, notes, symptom logs, and Q&A to prepare better clinician discussions.

The workflow may include:

- decoding user-owned DICOM/MRI series into high-resolution PNG/JPEG review images;
- organizing exported images by study, series, and view;
- creating contact sheets or selected-image packs for consultation prep;
- asking the chosen LLM/vision model to provide an explanatory, non-clinical review of visible patterns;
- generating better questions for radiologists, surgeons, physiotherapists, or other qualified clinicians.

The image-review text is generated by the user's chosen LLM or vision model/runtime. Spine Harmony does not validate that model, certify its outputs, or convert those outputs into clinical advice. Imaging review must not be used instead of radiologist reporting, clinician interpretation, diagnosis, or treatment planning. Avoid wording such as “the MRI proves,” “you have,” “you need,” or “this is safe/reliable/clinically validated.”

DICOM files and derived images may contain personal medical information. Do not commit real imaging files, converted images, metadata dumps, or reports containing real patient information to a public repository.

Concrete conversion instructions and a reference export utility are included in [`docs/imaging-workflow.md`](docs/imaging-workflow.md) and [`tools/dicom-export/`](tools/dicom-export/).

## Interactive Q&A

One of Spine Harmony's core patterns is structured back-and-forth Q&A. Users can ask the agent questions about their own spine-health notes, appointment summaries, imaging-report text, symptom logs, and draft reports.

Good Q&A use cases include:

- clarifying what a clinician or report appears to say;
- identifying contradictions or missing information in the record;
- comparing options discussed by clinicians;
- converting confusing source material into better follow-up questions;
- preparing for appointments through iterative question refinement;
- maintaining continuity across multiple conversations and documents.

Q&A outputs are for personal organization and clinician-discussion preparation only. They are not diagnosis, treatment advice, emergency triage, or clinical decision-making. A Spine Harmony agent must never tell a user to start/stop medication, delay care, ignore red flags, accept/reject surgery, or overrule a clinician.


## Symptom and pain tracking

Symptom and pain tracking is a core Spine Harmony feature. The agent can maintain a structured private diary that turns short user check-ins into a longitudinal trend record for clinician discussion.

Useful tracking patterns include:

- daily or scheduled pain check-ins using a 0–10 scale;
- morning/evening or once-daily status prompts;
- sleep, medication, activity, sitting/standing/walking tolerance, therapy, exercise, and heat/ice usage;
- flare, spasm, neurological-symptom, or new-symptom flags;
- post-procedure recovery diaries, such as tracking response after a spine injection or other clinician-directed intervention;
- trend tables that distinguish raw user-reported facts from cautious interpretation;
- summary handoffs before follow-up appointments.

The goal is not to diagnose the pain source, prove treatment response, or decide treatment. The goal is to preserve accurate daily facts, show cautious patterns over time, and help users ask better questions of qualified clinicians.

Full guidance, templates, and synthetic examples are included in [`docs/symptom-pain-tracking.md`](docs/symptom-pain-tracking.md), [`templates/symptom-tracker.md`](templates/symptom-tracker.md), and [`examples/anonymized-symptom-tracking.md`](examples/anonymized-symptom-tracking.md).


## Report structure and HTML style

Spine Harmony reports should be structured, source-grounded, and visually clean. The repo includes explicit guidance for modular patient-facing reports with user-specific headings, such as executive summary, patient profile, longitudinal timeline, symptom mapping, imaging findings, official radiology report, clinical audit, procedure-specific analysis, doctor discussion points, prioritized action plan, and sources.

Use [`REPORTS.md`](REPORTS.md), [`docs/report-generation.md`](docs/report-generation.md), and [`templates/report.html`](templates/report.html) as the baseline. Reports should use a restrained, professional HTML style: white background, strong hierarchy, card-based sections, clear caveats, print-aware CSS, and no decorative clutter.

## Runtime compatibility

Spine Harmony is designed to be **runtime-agnostic**. It can be adapted to agent systems that support persistent instructions, workspace files, and tool use.

Example target runtimes include:

- Hermes Agent;
- OpenClaw;
- Claude Code / Claude-based coding-agent workflows;
- other local or hosted AI-agent runtimes with equivalent file/tool access.

Claude Code / Claude Cowork compatibility note: this repository can be used as a project instruction/workspace pack for Claude by placing the core instructions in a project-level `CLAUDE.md` file and keeping private patient files outside version control. ## Privacy, LLM submission, and local data model

The public repository contains only generic instructions, templates, and synthetic examples. It does **not** contain real medical records, imaging, transcripts, or patient identifiers.

However, actual use of Spine Harmony will often involve submitting sensitive medical information to whatever AI/runtime provider the user chooses. If you upload or paste medical records, MRI/DICOM-derived images, radiology reports, clinician letters, symptom logs, or generated reports into an LLM, vision model, hosted agent, chat service, cloud IDE, or cloud storage workflow, that information is **not remaining purely local**. It may be transmitted to and processed by third-party systems according to their own terms, retention rules, training policies, logging practices, and security controls.

A local workspace protects you from accidentally publishing private files in this repository. It does **not** by itself guarantee privacy from your chosen model provider or runtime. Users should assume that medical records and imaging shared with an external AI service are being disclosed to that service, and should only do so if they understand and accept the privacy implications.

Do not commit:

- medical records;
- imaging files;
- converted MRI/DICOM images or contact sheets;
- imaging metadata dumps;
- doctor letters;
- appointment transcripts;
- symptom/pain diaries or check-in logs containing real user data;
- personal identifiers;
- API keys or tokens;
- exported reports containing real patient data.

## Hosted deployments

Do not deploy Spine Harmony as a public hosted chatbot, patient-facing service, clinical workflow, or tool that accepts real medical records unless appropriate legal, privacy, regulatory, clinical safety, and security review has been completed for the jurisdiction and use case. The open-source repository is a template/specification; hosted medical use is a separate risk category.
