1. Executive summary

A detailed front-page summary for readers who need the full picture before the specialist sections.

Current working picture

Cervical symptoms with mixed mechanical and neurological features

Maya R., a fictional 52-year-old school administrator in Bristol, has an 18-month pattern of neck pain with intermittent right arm tingling, episodic grip fatigue, and sleep disruption. Symptoms cluster around desk work, driving, and sustained neck extension. The fictional record set raises a structured clinical question: how much of Maya’s symptoms are from cervical nerve-root irritation, facet-mediated neck pain, myofascial guarding, shoulder contribution, or peripheral nerve entrapment?

The report does not decide that question. It organizes the evidence so Maya can ask the clinician better questions and avoid compressing a complex history into a rushed appointment narrative.

patient-side organizationuncertainty preservedclinician review needed

Decision support output

What the next consultation needs to resolve

  • Whether the MRI-described right foraminal narrowing plausibly matches the symptom map.
  • Whether exam findings point to C6, C7, another level, shoulder pathology, or peripheral nerve entrapment.
  • Whether conservative care has been adequate in duration, intensity, and adherence before escalation.
  • Whether further tests would change management: updated MRI, flexion-extension X-rays, EMG/NCS, diagnostic injection, or shoulder/nerve assessment.
  • Which symptoms would make Maya contact the clinic urgently rather than waiting for routine follow-up.

Core medical boundary

Spine Harmony is not diagnosing Maya, choosing surgery, ranking doctors, or clearing a procedure. It is a patient-memory and clinical-preparation layer: it consolidates records, separates facts from interpretation, identifies contradictions, drafts questions, and keeps a clear “verify with your clinician” boundary.

Why reliability grading matters

A patient-facing report becomes dangerous if it flattens all data into one confident story. The source index lets the reader see what is observed, what is inferred, and what must be confirmed by a clinician.

Right-sided lower neck ache, typically 3–6/10, worsened by sustained posture and extension. Improves with movement breaks, heat, and walking.

  • Supports discussion of mechanical contributors.
  • Does not distinguish disc, facet, muscle, or referred pain.
  • Needs physical exam and clinician correlation.

Fictional MRI report extract

Cervical spine MRI — March 2026

  • Mild straightening of cervical lordosis.
  • C5/C6: disc osteophyte complex with right foraminal narrowing, described as moderate.
  • C6/C7: shallow posterior disc bulge without severe canal stenosis.

  • No acute fracture, mass, or infection described in the fictional report.
What the agent can do

Translate report language into questions

The agent can explain that foraminal narrowing is a narrowing where nerve roots exit, but it must not claim that the MRI finding causes Maya’s symptoms. It can ask whether the right-sided narrowing fits the right arm pattern and whether the clinician sees any mismatch.

Recommended phrasing: “This may be relevant and needs clinical correlation.”

Imaging boundary

Patient-side AI can label, sort, and summarize imaging-related documents. It should not present vision-model observations as measurements, radiology interpretation, diagnosis, surgical indication, or clearance.

12. Specialist question list

The question list makes the appointment more useful without pushing the clinician toward a predetermined intervention.

Diagnosis confidence

Questions about what is actually causing symptoms

  1. What is your leading explanation for the neck and right-arm symptoms?
  2. How well do the MRI findings match the symptom distribution and your exam?
  3. Do you see objective weakness, reflex change, sensory loss, or signs of myelopathy?
  4. Is this more consistent with C6, C7, another level, peripheral nerve entrapment, shoulder contribution, or a mixed picture?
  5. Which parts of the history make you less confident?
Management sequence

Questions about what to do next

  1. What conservative options remain reasonable and for how long?
  2. What should Maya stop, modify, or continue?
  3. Would EMG/NCS, updated MRI, or diagnostic injection change management?
  4. If injection is considered, what is the exact target, medication, purpose, and success criterion?
  5. What would make surgery appropriate, inappropriate, or premature?
Risk and safety

Questions about escalation thresholds

  1. Which symptoms should trigger same-day medical contact?
  2. What degree of weakness or numbness is concerning?
  3. How should Maya distinguish a routine flare from a meaningful neurological change?
  4. Are there medication or activity risks specific to her context?
Decision question

Family-member framing

“If I were your family member with this pattern, what would you advise me to do next — and what would you definitely not do yet?”

14. Safety boundaries and escalation

A spine-focused patient agent must make safety boundaries conspicuous and conservative.

Urgent review

Neurological progression

New or worsening arm/hand weakness, coordination problems, gait changes, falls, or signs of spinal cord involvement.

do not wait
Emergency symptoms

Systemic or severe signs

Fever with severe spine pain, unexplained weight loss, cancer/infection concern, major trauma, or severe unrelenting pain.

same-day care
Agent boundary

No remote clearance

Spine Harmony can organize the record and flag questions. It cannot perform a neurological exam or clear treatment.

clinician decides

Emergency disclaimer

This sample report is not emergency guidance. A real patient with new severe or rapidly worsening symptoms should contact their clinician or local emergency services according to local medical advice.
