## User question

I converted my own lumbar MRI series into high-resolution review images. The model seems to notice one lower-lumbar level that looks different from the others. How should I use that observation?

## Spine Harmony-style answer

Treat the model's observation as a prompt for clinician discussion, not as a radiology finding or diagnosis. The useful next step is to compare three sources:

1. the formal radiology report;
2. the clinician's interpretation and physical examination;
3. your symptom pattern and timeline.

Questions to ask a qualified clinician:

- Which level does the formal report identify as clinically relevant, if any?
- Does the visual difference match my symptoms and examination?
- Could the finding be incidental or age-related?
- Are there signs that would change management, such as nerve compression, instability, inflammatory change, or another pain generator?
- Should any additional imaging views, reports, or measurements be reviewed?

This Q&A is for consultation preparation only. The LLM/vision model's image observations are not radiology interpretation, diagnosis, validated measurement, or treatment guidance.
